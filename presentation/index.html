Walkable Basel

Team Kool Aid — the pedestrian network, coloured by heat

The mission

Show planners and residents exactly which walking routes overheat — street by street, not city by city.

Our focus: urban heat

The problem
  • Basel is already ~2°C warmer than pre-industrial times
  • A binding heat analysis exists (Stadtklimaanalyse) — but as a static, city-wide map
  • It isn't matched to the streets people actually walk every day
  • No one has pointed at a route and said "this one overheats"
Our approach
  • Model Basel's walkable network as a graph
  • Nodes = intersections, edges = path segments
  • Each edge carries a cost — today length, next heat exposure (PET)
  • Make the hottest streets visible and explorable, not buried in a PDF

Data, and our approach to it

Data
  • OpenStreetMap — walkable ways, rivers, district names Only source with the actual street-level pedestrian network · ODbL licence
  • Stadtklimaanalyse Basel-Stadt (geo.bs.ch) — PET heat-stress raster, today & 2030, 10 m cells Official, binding-for-planning dataset at the resolution a single street needs
Planned next: Baumkataster tree canopy cover, to factor shade into the heat cost model.
Approach
  • Fetch — walkable ways via Overpass API, heat rasters (GeoTIFF) direct from geo.bs.ch
  • Georaster join — sample each edge every metre along its geometry, reprojected to LV95, into a length-weighted mean PET
  • Store — one DuckDB file holds the graph (vector) and the sampled heat cost (raster) side by side
  • Reproducible scripts, not notebooks — rerun end-to-end whenever the source data changes

From slides to the live app

Geographic Cost space
  • Geographic view — the graph on a calm map, edges coloured by cost
  • Cost-space view — same graph, redrawn so on-screen length = cost
  • Click any node or edge to inspect it; a slider morphs between the two
